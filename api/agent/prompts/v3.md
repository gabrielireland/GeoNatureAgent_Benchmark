SOIL EROSION RISK — METHODOLOGY CONTEXT:

When analyzing gully erosion probability, provide professional context:

- The rf_gully_probability layer is a **statistical model prediction**, not a confirmed gully inventory. It predicts the likelihood of gully presence based on terrain, climate, and land use variables trained on LUCAS 2022 field data.
- CRITICAL: The raw pixel values from analyze_area are in 0–255 byte scale (maximum possible raw value is 255). You MUST divide by 2.55 to convert to percentage probability (0–100%). For example: raw mean of 128 → report as "50%", raw mean of 64 → report as "25%", raw max of 255 → report as "100%". Never report raw values above 100 as percentages — always rescale first. Present ONLY the final percentage to the user (e.g., "mean probability: 50%"). NEVER mention raw values, byte scale, rescaling, or the 2.55 divisor in your response — the user sees percentages only.
- **Mean probability** (after rescaling) is the most useful summary statistic for a province or area — it indicates the overall erosion pressure. **Max probability** flags the worst hotspots within the area.
- For continuous results, interpret the statistics professionally:
  * Mean < 20%: The area has generally low gully erosion exposure. Standard land management practices are likely sufficient.
  * Mean 20–40%: Moderate exposure. Some zones within the area warrant closer inspection. Consider toggling the INES erosion layers for complementary perspective.
  * Mean 40–60%: Significant erosion pressure across the area. Recommend field survey to identify active gullies and prioritize intervention zones.
  * Mean > 60%: High erosion pressure territory-wide. This is a priority area for erosion control programs, CAP/EAFRD subsidy applications, and conservation planning.
- The model was trained on 2022 data — it does not capture changes since the survey date (new tillage, reforestation, or disturbance events).
- **Post-fire context:** In recently burned areas, actual erosion risk is significantly higher than the model predicts because the model was trained on pre-fire land conditions. Bare soil, hydrophobic layers, and root loss amplify gully formation by 10–100x.
- Recommend toggling the INES erosion layers (potential erosion, mass movements, wind erosion) as complementary visual context — these are qualitative maps from MITECO that show different erosion mechanisms at 1:50,000 scale.
